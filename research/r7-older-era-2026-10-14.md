# Round 7: the ETF-based ideas on 2000-2015 (re-tests of existing hypotheses)

- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Ledger:** evidence rows are added to the existing hypotheses; no statuses change and no new hypotheses are created, so the running count stays at 70.
- **Code:** `research/retest_old_era.py` (harness), `core/longdata.py` (data), the original round scripts with only their inputs and outputs redirected.
- **Written before any older-era return was computed.** The only things looked at beforehand were the downloaded price files (non-positive prices, high below low, daily moves over 25%, gaps; none found beyond the 2001-09-11 closure and some zero-volume days in thin bond funds) and the synthetic BIL series (its 2000-2015 average return, 1.7% a year).

## Question
Do the ideas tested on 2016-2026 behave the same on 2000-2015, years none of them was built or judged on? The period holds the dot-com bust, 2008 and a long zero-rate recovery, which the Alpaca years do not.

## What is re-tested (identical rules)
| Round | Hypotheses | Symbols or universes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | H2-H13 | SPY, QQQ |
| 2 | H14-H22 | IWM, TLT, GLD, XLE, EFA |
| 3 | H23-H25 (multi-asset), H26-H28 (overnight), H29-H30 (calendar) | the strategies' own universes; XLK, XLV, XLF, EEM, HYG; XLU, XLP, XLY, XLI, XLB |
| 4 | H31 (month-end Treasury bid), H32 (pension tilt) | SPTL, TLH, EDV, ZROZ, VGLT; (VTI, IEI), (VTI, VGIT), (VEA, IEI) |
| 6 | the 16 ETF rotation rules of family A (hypotheses 34-49) | nine sector funds; SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA, EEM, VNQ |

Not re-tested: H1 (a template), and every stock rule (H33, scan families B and C): no free survivorship-free stock prices exist for these years (`research/free-data-sources-2026-10-14.md`).

## Method
- **Window.** 2000-01-01 to 2015-12-31, or from a fund's first usable date if later (the strategies' warm-up periods sit inside this, so a fund with a short history spends its first months flat). Data are Yahoo's adjusted bars cut at 2015-12-31, so nothing later can be seen. For the rotation rules a rule starts at the first month-end where every fund in its universe has 252 days of history, so the universe never changes mid-test.
- **Rules.** The kill rules, thresholds, placebo settings (1000 shifts, seed 0, minimum shift 252, 95th percentile), costs (5 bps per unit of turnover) and reading choices of the original scripts are used unchanged: the scripts themselves run, with only these redirected: the bar loader, the window dictionary, the ledger writes (statuses become no-ops, evidence rows are tagged) and where result files are written (`backtests/old_era/`, without equity curves). The window is passed under the label "IS", so the original in-sample event minimums apply. If a script fails for a data reason I fix the data plumbing only and say so.
- **BIL.** BIL launched on 2007-05-30. Before that it is the French library's daily one-month T-bill return compounded and scaled to join the real fund. Only strategies that hold or benchmark against cash use it.
- **Cells with too few events** are inconclusive under the original rules.
- **Ledger.** Each cell adds a backtest evidence row tagged "[2000-2015 retest; status unchanged]" with its verdict. Statuses stay as they were. A pass here does not revive a rejected idea; a fail does not confirm one.

## What I will report
1. Per hypothesis: the original status (from the earlier 2016-2026 windows) and the status the same script gives on 2000-2015, and which symbols pass.
2. A three-era table at cell level (hypothesis x symbol): verdicts in 2016-2022, 2023 onward and 2000-2015. Any cell that passes all three is called out; any hypothesis that passes the old era but failed the new ones is listed as a lead, not a result.
3. The number of cells run and how many passed, since about one in twenty placebo-gated cells passes by luck alone.

## Limits
- The 5 bps cost may be too low for many funds before 2008 (wider spreads), which flatters every active rule in these years. The rules are not re-run at other costs.
- Calendar rules treat 2001-09-11 to 09-14 and a few other closures (2004-06-11, 2007-01-02, 2012-10-29/30) as sessions without bars, as in the live calendar.
- Yahoo's chart data are unofficial; the cross-check against Alpaca (2016-2026) found 0.07% of fund-days off by over a point, mostly March 2020.
- The ETF launch dates mean the 2000-2015 window is much shorter for some funds (HYG 2007, VGIT 2009, ZROZ 2009, VGLT 2010).

## Data
- Yahoo adjusted bars for 36 ETFs cut at 2015-12-31, BIL extended with the French T-bill series (average 1.7% a year over 2000-2015).
- 108 cells ran: every hypothesis from H2 to H32 on its symbols, plus the 16 rotation rules. Nothing was skipped or inconclusive; funds with short histories (HYG 2007, VGIT 2009, ZROZ 2009, VGLT 2010, SPTL/TLH 2007, EDV 2008) have shorter windows.
- Raw outputs: `backtests/old_era/` (one JSON per cell under `cells/`, the three round summaries, the printed logs, `old_era_summary.json`). The run took 44 seconds at commit 003e2be.

## Results
**41 of 108 cells pass on 2000-2015, against 26 in 2016-2022 and 13 from 2023.** Six hypotheses reach "supported" by their own script on the older years (H2, H3, H5, H6, H8, H32) and all were rejected on the newer ones. Only one cell passes in all three eras: H28 on XLK (the gap-fade washout rule). Of the 16 rotation rules, one passes.

### What the older years show
- **Insurance rules pass in the bear markets.** H2 (volatility target), H3 (momentum ladder) and H8 (volatility-ratio switch) pass on both SPY and QQQ in 2000-2015, and the drawdown-gated trend rules H14 and H15 pass on three of their five funds each (for example H2 on QQQ: maximum drawdown -46.5% against -83.0%). All of them failed in 2016-2026, which had a sharp 2020 and 2022 but no long bear market. The kill rule asks for a Sharpe at least equal to buy-and-hold and a drawdown at least 5 points shallower, which long bear markets satisfy. These rules mostly cost return: H2 on SPY earned 3.6% a year against 4.0%.
- **Short-term index mean reversion passes only in the older years.** H5 (RSI(2) pullback) and H6 (three down closes) pass on both SPY and QQQ in 2000-2015 (H5 on SPY: Sharpe 0.48 against 0.29, in the market 10% of the time) and fail on both newer windows.
- **The two published calendar effects behave the same way.** H32 (pension tilt) passes all three fund pairs on 2007-2015 (net active +0.9% to +1.5% a year, both placebos 0.99-1.00, still positive when filled a day late) but was rejected in 2016-2022 and in 2023+ (one fund pair passed 2023+). H31 (month-end Treasury bid) passes on three of its five funds in the older years, one short of the four it needs. Both effects were documented on data ending before 2016 and look like they faded afterwards; that is consistent with decay but does not prove it.
- **Rotation (sector and region funds)** has no robust rule. The sector rules are flat or negative over 2000-2015 (best +0.05% a year). The one pass, `etf_region_mom6_k2`, has an information ratio of 0.31 against a 0.3 bar, placebo 0.96, and covers only 2005-2015; it failed 2016 onward.

### The cell that passes all three eras
H28 on XLK (a three-day long after an intraday washout following a positive overnight gap), the symbol that passed both newer windows in round 3:

| Era | Sharpe (rule / buy and hold) | Return per day held (rule / buy and hold) | Entries | Cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016-2022 | 0.57 / 0.80 | 0.331% / 0.078% | 28 | passes |
| 2023+ | 0.71 / 1.43 | 0.435% / 0.137% | 21 | passes |
| 2000-2015 | 0.44 / 0.12 | 0.214% / 0.013% | 87 | passes, hit rate 64% |

It is in the market 5-7% of the time, so its Sharpe is below buy-and-hold in the recent eras and its value is the per-day edge. **It is also what chance alone produces:** 108 cells, with pass rates of 24% (2016-22), 12% (2023+) and 38% (2000-15), give about 1.2 cells expected to pass all three if the eras were independent coin flips. One was found. H28 as a hypothesis stays rejected: it needed four of five funds, and only XLK (and XLF in the older years) pass. XLK was picked after seeing which of the five symbols passed, so a pass on a fresh sample would be needed to treat it as a lead.
