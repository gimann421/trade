# Round 68: lobbying spend (2026-11-05)

Ideas 3 and 4 of the second Opus idea list (`/mnt/project-files/research/opus-new-ideas-round2-ranked-2026-11-05.md`, requested by Garret 2026-11-05) as hypotheses 286 and 287 (285 went to the segment-focus rule of round 69, registered first while this data was downloading). 285 were tested before this round, so a lucky pass is more likely than for a single test. Bar unchanged from round 25.

**This section is written before any result of these tests was computed.** No return of either rule was looked at before writing it.

## Data
Senate lobbying disclosures (lda.gov API, no key), quarterly reports 2016 to 2026. For each current member, all filings whose client name contains the member's SEC name are fetched, and only those whose normalised client name (case, punctuation and company suffixes removed) equals the normalised SEC name or one of its three most recent former names are kept. **No hand check of misses is done** (the idea list suggested one), so some members with differently written client names, or lobbying done through a subsidiary, are missed: their spend is understated or zero. Quarterly spend of a company = for each (registrant, quarter), the in-house `expenses` if reported, else the outside firm's `income` from the client, using the latest filing (amendments replace originals) posted by the decision day, summed over registrants. A quarter counts only from the date its filing was posted. Members with no matching filing in any year are unscored (cannot be told apart from true zero spenders).

## Rules (top 20% of scored names, equal weight, benchmark equal-weight scored names)
- 285 `lobbyintensity_quintile`: spend over the four calendar quarters whose last quarter ends on or before 20 days before the decision day (a quarter with no matching filing counts as zero) divided by revenue of the latest 10-K filed before the decision day and ending within 455 days (first-filed `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`, else `Revenues`, full-year duration). Hold the highest 20%.
- 286 `lobbygrowth_quintile`: spend of those four quarters divided by spend of the same four quarters a year earlier, minus 1, for names with at least $200,000 in the earlier year. Hold the highest 20%.
- Reported, not judged: mirrors; within-industry check for 285 (the idea list expects an industry bet), done by SIC-2 demeaned rank as for sales/price; correlation with the three passes; tech/health share.

## Common setup
Round 25-67 setup exactly: current S&P 500 members, monthly decisions from 2017-01-31 (lobbying data starts 2016 so a full trailing year exists by 2017-03), 5/10 bps, IS 2017-22, OOS 2023+, minimum 20 scored names.

## Bar, required in both windows
Net active >= 2.0%/yr, IR >= 0.3, placebo >= 0.95 (1000 draws, seed 0), net active > 0 at 10 bps, at least 48 / 36 rebalances. Correlation above 0.7 with an existing pass means the same bet.

## Multiple testing
Two rules, no variants. Total tested after this round: 287.

## Results (run 2026-11-05)

**Verdict: both rejected (hypotheses 286-287).** Tests after this round: 287. `backtests/round68_tests/`. Reference rules reproduce (low accruals +2.8/+3.2, R&D +5.5/+15.8, sales/price +4.5/+3.3). 33,622 matching lobbying filings. Coverage is thin: 104 names scored for 286 in 2017-18 rising to 282 by 2026 (213-232 by 2020-22), 132-220 for 287; the exact-name match without hand checks leaves many lobbyists out, as warned in the memo.

| Rule | IS 2017-22 | OOS 2023+ | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 286 highest lobbying spend over revenue | +1.3% (IR 0.26, pl 0.80) | +1.2% (IR 0.24, pl 0.76) | rejected: positive both windows, below the bar |
| 287 fastest lobbying growth | +1.2% (IR 0.26, pl 0.80) | +0.4% (IR 0.09, pl 0.59) | rejected |

Reported, not judged: 286 within-industry (SIC-2 percentile) IS -0.3%, OOS +2.1% (pl 0.85): no steady industry-neutral edge. 286 mirror (lowest spend) IS +0.9%, OOS -1.8%; 287 mirror (spending falling) IS -1.2%, OOS +0.2%. Correlation of 286 with low accruals -0.08, R&D 0.07, sales/price -0.27; 287: -0.06, 0.19, -0.42. Tech/health share 47% and 44%.

### Plain English
Companies that spend the most on lobbying relative to their size did a little better than average in both periods (about 1% a year), but far short of the 2% needed and no better than chance would often give. Companies whose lobbying spend was rising fastest did not do better. The match between company names and the lobbying register is imperfect, which makes any real effect harder to see. No pass.
