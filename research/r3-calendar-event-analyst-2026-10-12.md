Per instructions the two ideas below need no external dates (the calendar comes from `core.market_calendar` plus month arithmetic).

For a later round that can fetch https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm (2021-2027) and the historical pages for 2016-2020, the rule alignment is as follows. Literature (as reported in snippets, not read by me): Lucca and Moench (J. Finance, 2015) about 49 bp in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements since 1994; a 2018 NY Fed update says only press-conference meetings; Ignatieva and Ohashi (2025) say the drift fades. The effect is intraday (about 2pm ET on day A-1 to 2pm on announcement day A). In this backtester a weight set at close of day t is held over day t+1's close-to-close return. Setting weight 1 at the close of A-1 means the return of day A is earned: it contains the overnight A-1 close to A open and the morning to 2pm (most of the drift, about 2/3 of the 24h window), but also 2pm-4pm of day A, i.e. the announcement reaction itself, which is a large-variance, unsigned-in-expectation part that the effect does not cover, and it misses the 2-4pm part of A-1. Setting weight at close A-2 would also hold day A-1 (adds the missing 2-4pm piece) but doubles exposure to noise. The daily proxy therefore dilutes the signal with the announcement-day variance, and turnover costs (10 bps round trip per event, 5 bps each way) are large against a claimed 49 bp. Prior for sector ETFs: low.

## Hypotheses (round-1 lesson acknowledged: calendar rules went 0 for 12 cells; prior for both ideas is low)
Existing ledger entries consulted: H11 (turn-of-month), H12 (pre-holiday), H13 (opex week), all rejected on SPY/QQQ; H2-H22 rejected; H23-H28 proposed momentum/overnight ideas. Neither idea below repeats a tested rule.

### Shared test protocol (fixed now, no tuning)
- Symbols XLU, XLP, XLY, XLI, XLB; daily bars; windows IS 2016-01-01..2022-12-31, OOS 2023-01-01..2026-10-09; 5 bps per unit turnover; signals use full history.
- Alignment: weight w_t is computed at close t from the scheduled calendar (known in advance, no data used) and applied to the return of day t+1, as `generate_signals` plus the backtester's `shift(1)` does. "Next trading day" via `core.market_calendar.next_trading_day`.
- Per-cell metrics: RPED = return per exposure day (net of costs, as in `_metric`/`return_per_exposure_day`); BH = buy-and-hold mean daily return and Sharpe of the same window; CS = conditional Sharpe = mean/std (ddof=1) of the strategy's daily net returns on exposure days only, times sqrt(252).
- Cell verdict: events (entries) below the minimum -> "inconclusive". Otherwise "supports" iff all three conditions hold, else "contradicts".
- Symbol passes iff both windows "supports"; `symbol_status(cells, min_symbols=4)` must say supported; then placebo: `placebo_percentile(asset, position, 5.0, "return_per_exposure_day", n=1000, seed=0, min_shift=252)` computed on the full-history position vs full-history asset series per passing symbol (also per window if desired), mean percentile over passing cells >= 0.95. Metric: return_per_exposure_day (exposure differs from buy-and-hold, so Sharpe would conflate exposure with timing).
- Note: the five sector ETFs are highly correlated; 4 of 5 is closer to 2-3 independent looks. Passing is weak evidence even if it happens.
- Veto (hard reject, placebo not consulted): in any one window, if at least 3 of 5 symbols have RPED < BH mean daily return - 5 bps, the idea is rejected.

### Idea 1 (H29): Halloween window (Nov-Apr), all five sectors
- **Idea:** Sector ETFs earn their excess return in Nov-Apr, so holding only in that half beats buy-and-hold per exposure day.
- **Rule:** w_t = 1 iff month(next scheduled trading day after t) in {11,12,1,2,3,4}, else 0. Enter at the last October close, exit at the last April close. Exposure about 50%. No parameters except the month set, taken from the literature, not the data.
- **Why it might work:** Bouman and Jacobsen (2002, AER) and Jacobsen and Visaltanachoti (2009, sector-level, stronger in cyclicals than defensives) report a Nov-Apr premium; candidates: summer vacation lowering risk-bearing capacity, liquidity and fund-flow seasonality. Counterparties: investors who stay invested in summer and bear its lower compensation. Cited from memory and snippets, not verified here. Honest caveat: widely published since 2002, so decay is likely, and my expectation is that XLU/XLP fail (defensives), leaving at most 3 cyclicals, which cannot reach 4 of 5. I am proposing it because it has a documented cause and tests the flagship month-of-year effect round 1 did not.
- **Difference from H11-13:** a six-month window, 1 entry per year, versus a few days per month.
- **Event counts (dates only, from the calendar on these data):** IS 7 full Oct-end entries (2016-2022) plus the Jan-Apr 2016 winter in progress at the start; exposure days IS 864 of 1762. OOS: 3 full entries (Oct 2023, 2024, 2025) plus Jan-Apr 2023 carried from Dec 2022; exposure days 450 of 946. Minimum: 7 full entries IS, 3 OOS (the OOS is thin, 3.3 winters; any OOS "support" is weak).
- **Conditions needed:** (a) RPED >= BH mean daily return + 3 bps AND RPED >= +2 bps absolute (guard for a flat or negative buy-and-hold: the additive margin applies to a negative BH too, the absolute floor stops "beating" a bad benchmark with a still-negative return); (b) CS >= BH Sharpe + 0.10. The 3 bps margin is about half the literature's size scaled to half-year exposure (roughly 6-10 pts a year over about 126 days is 5-8 bps a day), rounded down.
- **Proven wrong if:** fewer than 4 of 5 symbols have both windows supporting; or the veto above triggers; or placebo mean percentile over passing cells < 0.95 (the shifted position keeps the same 6-month exposure but at other times of the year, so this directly tests whether Nov-Apr is special).

### Idea 2 (H30): Earnings-season window (trading day 11 to month-end in Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct)
- **Idea:** Sector ETFs earn a higher return per day during peak corporate earnings-reporting weeks than on average.
- **Rule:** w_t = 1 iff the next trading day after t is trading day number >= 11 of a month in {1,4,7,10} (counting trading days within the month, from the scheduled calendar), else 0. Enter at close of trading day 10, exit at the last trading day's close of that month. Exposure about 17%, 4 entries a year. Parameters fixed by reasoning: most S&P 500 firms report starting about two weeks after quarter end and mostly finish by month end; day 11 and month end are round choices, not tuned.
- **Why it might work:** Savor and Wilson (2016) (as I recall, not verified) find that stocks earn a premium in periods of scheduled earnings announcements, as announcements resolve systematic uncertainty that investors demand compensation for; a sector ETF is a basket of reporters. Other side: investors who avoid holding through announcement-heavy periods (risk-averse, event-avoiding, or hedging). Honest caveat: the evidence is stock-level announcement-day returns, not an index-level season effect, and sector ETFs mix reporters at different dates; it may well be absent.
- **Difference from H11-13:** the window is defined by the quarterly reporting cycle, not month boundaries, holidays or option expiry. Overlaps stated: the last 1-2 days of the window coincide with H11's days -2/-1, and the third-Friday option expiry week falls inside (H13), so some overlap with rejected ideas exists.
- **Event counts (dates only):** IS 28 entries, 304 exposure days; OOS 15 entries (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct 2023-2025 plus Jan, Apr, Jul 2026; Oct 2026 entry not yet occurred), 168 exposure days. Minimum: 24 entries IS, 12 OOS.
- **Conditions needed:** (a) RPED >= BH mean daily return + 4 bps AND RPED >= +3 bps absolute; (b) CS >= BH Sharpe + 0.15. The margin is set above Idea 1's because exposure is only 17%, so a real premium should show up as larger per-day return; 4 bps is about the buy-and-hold daily mean itself (roughly doubling it), a modest bar for an effect that is claimed to be material.
- **Proven wrong if:** fewer than 4 of 5 symbols pass both windows; or the veto above triggers; or the placebo (return_per_exposure_day, shifted position keeps ~17% exposure and 4 entries a year at other times) mean percentile over passing cells < 0.95.

## Why it might work / How it could fail / Test plan
See each idea above (the per-idea sections hold the mechanisms, kill thresholds and the common test plan). Benchmarks: buy-and-hold of the same ETF in the same window plus the circular-shift placebo. Costs 5 bps per unit turnover. Both ideas share a reason to fail: a published calendar effect that is a minority of annual returns, tested on about 7 plus 3.3 years, with correlated symbols.
