# Round 17: the two round 16 leads (2026-10-17)

Garret said "Research the hints. And then test" on 2026-10-17. Research: `research/deep-research-round4b-leads-2026-10-17.md` (project folder), which put the odds at about
6% (10-day ETF reversal) and 3% (least-similar filings). Hypotheses 145 and 146; 144 were tested before this round.

**This section is written before any result of these checks was computed.**

## What kind of test this can be
Both leads were found by looking at the 2023-on results of round 16, so that window cannot confirm them. No earlier era is reachable: Alpaca
returns ETF bars only from January 2016, Yahoo and Stooq are blocked, Alpha Vantage history is a paid endpoint, and the pre-2016 stock prices for
filings need the Sharadar data. So this round tests **robustness in the data we have**, not independence:
- Is the lead one isolated spike among its neighbours (a sign of luck from picking the best of several variants), or a plateau (a sign of something real)?
- Does it hold in both halves of the 2023-on window?

A plateau does **not** make a rule supported: it leaves the status at "testing" and sends the lead to a second era (Sharadar for filings, a new data source for ETFs). Only a spike
gets "rejected". No shadow or paper change comes from this round.

## Test 1: 10-day ETF reversal (hypothesis 145), `rev10s_h3_band40_etf`
As round 16 test 2 (same 169-ETF list, eligibility, 2 bps primary and 5 bps stress, band rule, every 3 sessions, benchmark), with the volatility-scaled return measured over **k = 5, 8, 10, 12, 15 and 20 sessions**.
- **Plateau if all of these hold:** for each of k = 8, 10, 12 and 15, OOS (2023 on) net active >= 2.0%/yr at 2 bps, OOS information ratio >= 0.3 and OOS net active at 5 bps > 0; **and** for k = 10 the OOS selection placebo >= 0.95 and net active > 0 in both halves of OOS (2023-2024 and 2025 on) at 2 bps.
- Otherwise: spike, rejected. In-sample (2017-2022) numbers and k = 5 and 20 are reported, not required.

## Test 2: least-similar filings (hypothesis 146), `filing_changer_q_m`
As round 16 test 1 (same scores, covered set, benchmark, monthly, 5 bps, 100-day age limit), holding the **least** similar names. Six variants: cosine and Jaccard similarity, each for the least-similar 10%, 20% and 33%.
- **Plateau if:** at least 4 of the 6 variants have OOS net active >= 2.0%/yr, OOS information ratio >= 0.3, net active > 0 at 10 bps, and net active > 0 in both halves of OOS (2023-2024 and 2025 on).
- Otherwise: spike, rejected. In-sample numbers are reported; the published pattern (stable names win) and round 16 test 1 already say the in-sample window is flat or negative, so a plateau would be a post-2023 regime story only.

## Multiple testing
Eleven numbers feed these two rules in total. The criteria above are fixed in advance, and a plateau only passes the lead on to a second era.

## Results (run 2026-10-17)

**Verdict: both leads are spikes, rejected (hypotheses 145, 146).** Nothing is added to the shadow or paper records. Hypotheses tested after this round: 146.
`backtests/round17_leads/`. This is a robustness check on the same data that suggested the leads, not an independent test.

Plain English: the 10-day rule is the best-looking of six horizons, and its neighbours do not agree: 12 days earns about nothing and 8 days lost money in 2023 and 2024. Only 10-day holds
in both halves of the recent period, so it fails the plateau test (the 12-day neighbour misses the bar). It looks like the best of six tries, not a stable effect.
