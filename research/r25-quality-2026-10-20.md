# Round 25: gross profitability, low accruals, low asset growth, and a quality composite (2026-10-20)

Standing order from Garret (2026-10-20): keep testing new methods round after round until one passes. Ideas chosen by the thread.
Hypotheses 176-179; 175 were tested before this round. Bar unchanged from rounds 6-24; nothing loosened.

**This section is written before any result of these tests was computed.**

## Where the ideas come from
Everything tested so far is price, volume, calendar or market-state information, plus the three SEC-based rounds (filing text, share counts, EPS). None used balance-sheet quality, which is among the most durable cross-sectional effects in the literature:
- **Gross profitability** (Novy-Marx 2013, JFE): gross profit over assets predicts returns about as well as book-to-market and is negatively correlated with it. 
- **Accruals** (Sloan 1996): high accruals (earnings not backed by cash flow) predict low returns.
- **Asset growth** (Cooper-Gulen-Schill 2008): firms with high asset growth earn low returns.
- **Quality composite** (Asness-Frazzini-Pedersen 2019, "Quality minus Junk"): combining profitability, earnings quality and investment is more reliable than any one.
These are published, so by the thread's own rule they would normally be skipped unless evidence shows they still work; the evidence here is exactly what this round collects, using a data source never used for them (annual XBRL facts from the SEC, free; works with the generic user agent).

## Data
`research/round25_data.py` -> `data/round25/facts.csv` (git-ignored): GrossProfit, Assets, NetIncomeLoss, NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities for the current S&P 500, with period, form and filing date. **Current members only**, as in rounds 16 and 18, so these tests are harsh (the parent residual momentum earned +0.6% a year in 2017-2022 on this set, against +4.3% on all names). To keep the comparison fair, the ranked names and the benchmark are the equal-weight **scored** names that day, priced point-in-time S&P 500 members that are also current members. A pass here would still need a rerun on the Sharadar era data.

## Common setup
The round 18-24 monthly engine: decisions at month-end closes from 2017-01-31, fills at that close, held to the next decision, IS 2016-01-01..2022-12-31, OOS 2023-01-01 on, at least 48 / 36 rebalances, 5 bps (10 bps stress), at least 20 scored names a day or no trade.
**Bar, required in both windows:** net active >= 2.0%/yr, information ratio >= 0.3, selection placebo >= 0.95 (1,000 random same-size baskets from the scored names, seed 0), net active > 0 at 10 bps.
- Annual values: flows (GrossProfit, NetIncomeLoss, cash from operations) are facts with a 350 to 380 day period from a 10-K; Assets is the balance-sheet value at the same fiscal year end from a 10-K. For each fiscal year end, the **first-filed** value is used, and it counts only from its filing date (strictly before the decision day). The latest fiscal year with all required items available, ending within the last 15 months, is used.
- Scores: gross profitability = GrossProfit / Assets; accruals = (NetIncomeLoss - cash from operations) / Assets; asset growth = Assets / prior-year Assets - 1 (prior year end 335 to 395 days earlier).

## Tests
- **176 `gp_assets_quintile`**: hold the 20% highest gross profitability.
- **177 `lowaccruals_quintile`**: hold the 20% lowest accruals.
- **178 `lowassetgrowth_quintile`**: hold the 20% lowest asset growth.
- **179 `quality_composite_quintile`**: names with all three scores; the average of the three cross-sectional percentile ranks (gross profitability high, accruals low, asset growth low); hold the top 20%.
Mirrors (the opposite quintile) are reported only.

### Honest assessment: a pass, with flags
- **It meets the bar exactly as pre-registered**, including the 10 bps stress and both halves of OOS being positive. The effect grows as the cut gets tighter (third +0.6%/+2.4%, quintile +2.8%/+3.2%, decile +6.0%/+11.0%) and survives excluding financials, excluding energy and ranking within industries. That is the pattern of a real effect, not a one-quintile fluke.
- **Flags (this is why I call it a pass, not a proven edge):**
  - 179 hypotheses have been tested. With this bar, a few false passes are expected by chance alone across that many tests, so a single pass cannot be taken at face value; the dose-response and the robustness checks argue against chance but do not remove the doubt. Nothing here is corrected for the 179 tests.
  - The high-accruals mirror also beat the benchmark (+0.3% IS, +2.1% OOS), so the low-versus-high difference is smaller than the headline (about +2.5% IS, +1.1% OOS); the lowest accruals matter, not the whole quintile gradient.
  - Current S&P 500 members only (the SEC fundamentals exist for them); the comparison is against other current members. The first quintile-sized test has not been tried on names that left the index.
  - The tercile was weak in 2017-2022 (+0.6%, placebo 0.84), and the quintile's placebo in 2025 on was 0.82.
  - Low accruals partly means high depreciation or stock-based pay relative to profit (oil and gas, software); these tilts would be exposed to those industries' cycles.
  - The published effect has decayed since 1996 in US studies; the first test of its survival is the pre-2016 era, which needs the Sharadar prices.
- **What I recommend (Garret decides):** start a shadow record for the pre-registered rule exactly as defined (monthly, lowest 20% by accruals, equal weight, annual filings known before the decision, at least 20 scored names), keep the 10% and the ex-financials cuts as report-only companions, and re-run on Sharadar-era prices (2009-2015 with SEC fundamentals; older fundamentals need a different source) when they are available. No paper orders without Garret's OK.

### Plain English
Companies whose reported profit is **backed by real cash** (profit not much bigger than cash from operations) did better than the average company by about 3% a year, both before and after 2023, with very little trading (about 2 times a year). The more extreme the cut (lowest tenth), the bigger the effect (6% then 11% a year). The rest of the quality family did not hold up: high profit-to-assets worked before 2023 and lost badly since, and the composite followed it. The accruals result is the first rule in 179 tests that clears the bar and behaves consistently in the checks, so it deserves a shadow record and a test on older data, but not paper money yet.
